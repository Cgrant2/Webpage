Biomes: A major biotic community defined by local plant life and local climate
Differ by soil, temp, light, and altitude
They tend to occur in latitudinal bands, rows not columns

Tropical Rainforests:
Thin nutrient poor soils surprisingly, because the high productivity tends to
make the nutirents trapped inside of plants
High productivity/photosynthesis
Warm year round

Tropical Season Forests:
Pacific side of mexico and central america, as well as inland india
Have a distinct dry and wet season
Mix of woods and grasslands
Life has more adaptations to survive dry times
More disturbed by human activity because of the dry season existing

Tropical Savannah:
Nutrient poor soil, not great for agriculture
Wildfires are essential in this biome
Enough rainfall for trees, but generally dry
Seems to be near, but not in the equator, surrounding

Grasslands:
No trees,
Drier than savannahs
Fertile soil
We can find these in western America
Sub-type:
Prarie/Temperate Grassland
Has more moderate rainfall
Less than 4% remain in the US
So good for agriculture that they were plowed and now far fewer and smaller

Deserts:
Includes Antarctica

Temperate Shrublands:
Commonly forgotten
also called Chaparral
Often coastal
Have dry seasons
Commonly depend on fire for seeds to reproduce

Temperate Forest:
Temperate rainforests exist in places like the Pacific NW
Deciduous: Lose leaves in autumn
Coniferous/Pines: Retain needles

Boreal Forest:
Northernmost parts of the world, only coniferous trees
Also called Taiga
Formed about 12,000 Years ago
Caused by glaciers retreating north, revealing land good for growth
Lower species diversity
