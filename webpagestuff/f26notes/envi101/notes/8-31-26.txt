Species class

Within ecosystems we see many interactions betwen species

A species role in an ecosystem is its niche, can be very sepcific

Generalists: can exploit a wide variety of resources
Specialists: have a very narrow nice
Generalist: Raccoon, eat many things and can change easily
Specialist: Koala, can only eat eucalyptus

Interspecific Competition:
Between species using similar resources, ie squirrels and birds sharing 
tree resources

Intraspecific competition 
Among the same species, all competeting for the same resources, ie 
plants
is a more powerful evolutionary driver, more direct competition

Herbivory:
Consumption of plants by animals, great for animals, they get energy, 
bad for plants, they die X_X
Plants develop defenses against this, such as cactus spines, or poison in
berries

Predation:
Animals eating animals, great for predator, get energy, awful for prey, 
they die X_X, prey evolve to survive better, such as fish swimming in 
schools, turtles with shell defenses, and porcupines with spines

Coevolution can occur between predator and prey
Defenses against Predators:
Chemical defense: prey may develop poisons
Crptic Coloration: Blending in to environment
Flashing Colorations: Butterflies with eye patterns

Aposematism:
Used by animals that are toxic of have chemical defenses, Black Widows, 
Lionfish, Skunks, Etc.

Not all brightly colored animals are toxic, they might only try to look toxic
called mimicry

Batesian Mimicry: Harmless animal looking poisonous or otherwise dangerous
by mimicing more dangerous animals
EX: Ant-mimicking Jjumping spider
Mullerian Mimicry: Animal acts like a similarly looking, but unrelated
species
EX: Monarch and Viceroy Butterflies, Viceroy butterflies were thought to be
harmless in the past

Parasitism: Great for parasite get energy, bad for host, lose resources
Mites, Lice, Dog heartworm

We deal with them frequently in NY because of Ticks

Parasites dont neccesarrily kill the host

Parasites can reduce host fitness, they could stop reproduction, or 
sap energy
Good example is Common Cuckoo being a parasite to Reed Warblers

Defense mechanisms have coevolved with parasites:
Gooming: Cats licking their fur to remove parasites
Inflammatory response: Immune response
Scabs: Prevent more parasites from enering the bloodstream
Cysts in muscle or skin to isolate parasite to prevent more tissue damage

Symbiotic realtionships/Mutualism
Both parties benefit
examples include honeybees and flowers, bee gets material for reporduce, 
flowers also get help with reproduction
Coral Reefs Coral polyps protect/guard algae, algae produces energy for 
the coral

Commensalism:
One species benefits, one is uneffected, Whaletails and Barnacles, 
Hermit crabs using discarded shells or trash for shells

Species with Disproportionate Impact
Keystone Species:
Wolves, sea otters that eat urchins
Wolves help preserve an ecosystem by reducing populations of grazers
As do sea otters with aquatic environments
Ecosystem Engineers (Often considered a type of keystone species):
Beavers, Earthworms, Prarie Dogs
Beavers: Cut trees, build dams, may create ponds
Earthworms: Aerate Soil, change soil composition
Prarie Dogs: Tunnel undergorung, can be used by many species

Biodiverse:
Low biodiversity is less species, such as corn fields made by humans
High biodiversity includes coral reefs, has coral, fish, algae, mollusks, etc

Measures of species
Species richness, amount of different species, doesnt care about distrobution
Species diversity cares about relative abundance, amounts of each species

Two types of ecologicl succesion
* Primary Succession occurs in an area where no biotic community existed, 
species moving in to an empty place
Secondary: Previously occupied area that underwent a distubance being 
taken by new species
