Keystone species: A species that has a disproportionately large impact on its 
ecosystem relative to its abundance
Ex: 
* Purple Sea Star - First known keystone species, cruicial to maintaining 
biodiversity, removal led to other species populations growing rapidly
* Saguaro Cactus - Used by many desert animals for food and shelter
* Jaguar - Important in many tropical rainforests in order to keep many herbivores 
in check from overgrazing and overpopulating

Wolf eradication in the US
By 1958 most wolves were eradicated from most of the US and Mexico

The extermination campaign was very sucessful as we expanded west, we eradicated 
Bison, a common food source of wolves; they changed to cattle from ranches, so in 
the 1800s wolf poisonings began. The gov stepped in in the early 1900s, and created 
wolf bounties to reward those who killed wolves

We didnt consider how important they were to the ecosystem as top predators

Trophic cascade, we removed the top predator, led to cascading effects down the food
chain. In absence of wolves Elk exploded in population, which overgrazed the 
ecosystem because they had no checks, vegetation declined. They grazed frequently 
near riverbanks, the rivers changed as roots were weakened, hurting aquatic 
ecosystems

By the late 1960s, scientists and conservationists started to advocate for wolves, 
knowing now how important they were for the ecosystem

Eventually 8 wolves were reintroduced to Yellowstone in 1995

Wolves reproduced and increased, decreasing the elk population, keeping them 
in check, increasing vegetation, Aspen tree height increases, Browsing decreased
More berries -> more bears
Taller trees -> more habitat for Songbirds and birds in general
Ravens and Bald Eagles feed on leftover carrion made by wolves
Beavers didnt have to compete as much for trees, beavers reduce stream erosion, 
creating more aquatic habitats

Successful reintroduction campaign
