Aristotle believed the world was made from the 4 elements

As tech has advanced we have been able to do scientific experiments 
to understand our world better

Conservation of mass - mass cannot be created or destroyed

We discovered that atoms have positive and negative charges, the negative 
coming from electrons, and positive from the center of the atom

Matter - anything with mass and space
Element - substance that cannot be broken down into other substances 
by chemical reactions

All elements are composed of atoms
Atomic Number - Total number of protons in an atom
Mass number - Total number of protons AND neutrons in the nucleus
Average Atomic Mass - Average weight of atoms of an element

Atoms exist in isotopes, same atomic number but different masses 
(coming from a difference in neutrons)

(Different carbon isotopes can be useful for carbon dating)
(Different uranium isotopes can be used for nuclear power, though most 
uranium on earth is the less useful type)

Molecules/Compounds - Two or more atoms of the same or different 
elements joined through chemical bonds

Organic Molecules - contains either Carbon-Hydrogen or Carbon-Carbon bond

Ion - Atoms or Molecules that have gained or lost a charge through 
gaining or losing electrons

Acid forms H+ ions are in water
Bases form OH- ions when dissolved in water

pH is logarithmics

Macromolecules - Large Organic Molecules
Polymers:
---------
Carbohydrates - our primary source of dietary energy, includes sugars
Monosaccharide - simple sugars, cant be broken down
Disaccharide - Constructed from 2 monosccharides, ie lactose
Polysaccharide - Chains of many mono- or di- saccharides, 
includes undigestible cellulose and digestable starch
Lipids - Hydrophobic (not cool), component if cell walls, includes fats, 
oils, and hormones, we burn some for energy such as propane
Proteins -  Composed of Amino Acids, many Amino acids exist, but only
22 are incorperated into proteins, proteins can catalyze chemical
reactions or form structural components of cells
(Further understanding of proteins has helped us develop things like
plant based meat)
Nucleic acids - store information and instructions on how to build protein,
includes DNA and RNA

Polymer applications: Sustainable Asphalt, increased use of plastics in 
roads, plastic that would otehrwise be dumped in landfills

Energy - the ability to do work (Flowing water, a driving car, electricity, 
heat)
includes Potential, Kinetic, and Chemical energy

Engines using Oxygen and Isooctane, both molecules rich in chemical
energy, burn producing Carbon Dioxide and Water, both molecules poor
in chemical energy

Laws of thermodynamics:
First law - Conservation of energy, energy is not created or destroyed, 
but rather moved and converted
Second law - As energy is used it becomes less useful; entropy increases in
natural systems

Energy efficiency: Measure of how much work results from each unit
of energy put into a system
